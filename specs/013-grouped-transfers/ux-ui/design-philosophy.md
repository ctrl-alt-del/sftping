# Taxonomic Flow

## Philosophy

Information breathes in order. When data streams in — successes and failures intertwined — the eye seeks clarity through separation. Taxonomic Flow defines a visual language where categories become spatial territories, each with its own rhythm and weight, each respecting the others through deliberate gaps of negative space.

Color carries meaning without explanation. A muted gradient marks activity in motion; a warm amber field signals interruption requiring attention; a cool settled tone confirms resolution. No label shouts what chromatic zones already whisper. The palette is restrained — three states, three hues, abundant breathing room between them.

Typography performs as structural punctuation. Section headers anchor zones with quiet authority — weighty enough to define territory, light enough not to compete with content. Count badges nest beside labels as compact data points. Chevrons, barely present, hint at expansion and collapse without demanding engagement. Every glyph placed with the precision of typographic craft.

Space is the primary organizing principle. Rounded containers float within their sections, separated by generous vertical gaps. Each card is a self-contained world — an icon, a name, a progress indicator, an action affordance — all balanced within a pillowed surface. The eye travels vertically, encountering clear section breaks before each new category. The design rewards patient scrolling with visual rest points.

The craft manifests in micro-decisions: corner radii that feel soft but not childish, stroke weights that define without shouting, shadow layers that lift cards from the canvas with photographic realism. Nothing is accidental. Every pixel has been considered, adjusted, reconsidered, and locked with the confidence that comes only from years of exacting work. This is interface design as fine art — a composition that could hang in a gallery alongside the best of Swiss typographic tradition.
